Comfort

Every other number in this project is what an attacker gets. This page is about the other axis: whether anyone can stand to look at it. Both panels below are live and running at your display's refresh rate, so this is the one thing you have to judge with your eyes rather than read off a table.

There is no comfort score here, on purpose. Producing one means inventing coefficients for a spatiotemporal contrast sensitivity function, and an invented coefficient inside a metric is worse than no metric, because it survives review by looking quantitative. What is measured below is modulation, which is real and mechanical. Whether a given amount of it is tolerable is a judgement, and it is yours.

Two kinds of flicker, and only one of them pays for itself

Within-pair is the modulation between the two frames of one cycle. That is the flicker the technique needs: it carries the masking and it cannot be reduced without reducing the protection with it.

Across-cycle is the modulation between the last frame of one cycle and the first of the next. It carries nothing. Every code level of it is churn a viewer sits through for no security at all, and it is the number worth attacking.

What is already settled

Re-randomising the noise on every cycle is correct, and it looked like an implementation detail for a long time. Freezing one pattern and inverting it leaks the same, 0.164 against 0.166 over 24 seeds, so it is free on security and the obvious thing to try.

It is also twice as bad to look at. Held fixed, the next cycle's first frame is the exact inverse of this one's last, so every pixel becomes a full-amplitude square wave at the cycle frequency: coherent in time, stable in space, which is the stimulus the visual system is most sensitive to. Across-cycle modulation goes from 80.6 to 161.3 for nothing in return.

Blue noise, which is free

White noise puts equal energy at every spatial frequency, including the low ones, and low spatial frequency is exactly where temporal sensitivity peaks. So part of a white-noise field is flicker you feel without being paid for it in masking. Blue noise pushes that energy toward the spatial limit instead, which is why printing and rendering have preferred it for decades.

The thing that had to be checked is whether a blur eats it, since a high-frequency bias is exactly what a blur removes. It does not, because the block size still dominates. Measured over 12 seeds at the shipped defaults:

Indistinguishable on security. But the comfort case did not come out the way the theory predicted, and the panels above measure it live: blue noise scores a slightly higher across-cycle modulation than white, around 52 against 48 at the defaults, while its raw leak comes out a little lower.

The spatial argument does not transfer to the temporal axis, and it should not have been assumed it would. Pushing noise energy to high spatial frequency is about where the eye resolves detail. Across-cycle modulation is about how much each pixel changes between one cycle and the next, and a high-passed field is not obliged to change less. Two different axes, and only one of them was reasoned about.

So blue noise is a small security win with a small churn cost, rather than the free comfort win it was proposed as. Whether the finer texture actually reads better is still a question for your eyes, which is why it is switchable and why white is still the default.

Still untested, and the trap in the biggest one

Weighting amplitude toward the ink is probably the largest comfort win available, since flicker over empty background protects nothing. It is also the easiest to get badly wrong: where the noise is, is where the text is. Pushed far enough, the amplitude map traces the glyphs and a single frame gives their shape away in the noise texture alone, without any value being recovered. It has to be a gentle weighting over a blurred ink map, never a mask, and the leak cost of each setting has to be measured rather than assumed.

Adapting to the measured refresh rate. refreshHz is already surfaced and the presets ignore it. At 120Hz the cycle runs at 60 and the whole trade moves, so the same preset is a different experience on different hardware.
